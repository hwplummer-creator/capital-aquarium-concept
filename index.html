What a visitor sees today

Five things worth fixing on the current site

These come from visiting capitalaquarium.com and checking its pages and code.

Fix firstPhone numbers don't dial

The two phone links on the site are written as tel+1-703-… instead of tel:+1703… (a missing colon). On a phone, tapping the number doesn't start a call. The "ext 2" service link also points to a different number (703-822-1963) than the one shown on the page (703-439-0700 ext 2).

Fix firstThe top banner appeared empty

When I loaded the homepage, the large area under the menu showed only a blank light-blue block, with no photo or message. It's the most valuable space on the page.

Missed chanceNo way to shop or book

The store sells fish, corals, food and top equipment brands, but the site has no product browsing or reservation. There's no booking form for consultations or service visits either. Customers have to phone.

Missed chanceHard to find on Google

The homepage has no meta description and no structured business data (hours, address and reviews that search engines can read). Three of the nine homepage images have no descriptive alt text.

DatedThe look and the details

The layout looks like it dates from the 2017 build (that's when the site's images were uploaded). Some images still load from a temporary transfer address rather than the main domain. The gallery page shows small thumbnails, so the best part of the business (the tanks) doesn't get to shine.

How it would be built

On WordPress, with standard tools

The site already runs on WordPress, so nothing needs to be rebuilt from scratch. The concept maps onto a modern theme, WooCommerce for the shop, and a handful of small plugins.

Try the working WordPress demo

Opens a working WordPress and WooCommerce mockup in your browser. The first load takes about a minute. Nothing is saved, and all items and prices are sample data.

Foundation

  • Theme: a fast block theme (Kadence, GeneratePress or Blocksy)
  • SEO: Rank Math or Yoast, for descriptions and local-business data
  • Speed: image optimizer (ShortPixel or Imagify) and caching (WP Rocket or LiteSpeed Cache)
  • Safety: UpdraftPlus backups, Wordfence security, and a staging copy for testing changes

What the owner would manage

  • Add or edit products in the normal WordPress dashboard
  • Mark items "New arrival" to feature them in the weekly row
  • Set stock counts, and the "Only N left" warnings follow automatically
  • Review reservation requests in one inbox, then confirm by phone or email

Biggest questionInventory accuracy

Live counts are only useful if they match the shelves. That depends on what the store uses at the register. With Square, an official WooCommerce sync keeps stock in step. With another system, there may be a connector, or staff could update stock by spreadsheet. This answer decides whether the project is simple or complicated.

Sensible startTreat livestock differently

Fish and corals are one-of-a-kind and perishable. Reserve-only avoids online checkout, shipping and live-arrival problems. Supplies can sell normally, and big-ticket gear can stay "Ask in store", as in the concept.

Plugin names are common, well-regarded options, not requirements. Features and pricing change, so these should be confirmed before any quote. Most of the core tools are free or inexpensive, so the main cost is setup time.
How it could happen

A phased approach, with low risk

The site runs on WordPress, so this can be done in steps without a risky all-at-once switch.

Quick fixes (about a day)

Repair the phone links and the empty banner, add a meta description and alt text. These help immediately.

New design

Apply the modern homepage, gallery, service pages and a booking request form for consultations and service visits.

Online shop

Add a product catalog and reservation list. Start with "reserve for pickup" so no online payments are needed.

Inventory feed

Connect stock counts and weekly arrivals to whatever the store uses to track inventory, or a simple dashboard the staff can update.

Open questions for the owner: Who manages the site now? How is inventory tracked today? Should animals be reservable online, or only supplies? Pricing for each phase depends on the answers.
